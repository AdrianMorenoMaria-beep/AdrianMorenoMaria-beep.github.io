About

I am a PhD candidate in Applied Economics at the Universitat Autònoma de Barcelona. I work in industrial organization and competition economics, with a focus on digital platforms, e-commerce and market power.

My job market paper studies what followed the European Commission’s 2017 Google Shopping decision. The Commission prohibited Google from favoring its own comparison shopping service in search results but left the design of compliance to Google. Using web traffic data and comparing European with non-European markets, the paper shows that the results page Google built in response did not restore the visibility of rival comparison shopping services, and that smaller rivals lost the most.

A related project studies competitive spillovers across advertising formats. On a Google results page, product ads (the Shopping boxes with a picture and a price) and text ads (the sponsored links) sit side by side and compete for the same shoppers, so who advertises in one format can change firms’ incentives to advertise in the other. Amazon’s exit from Google Shopping in July 2025 provides a natural test: after it left, more firms, especially manufacturers, started buying text ads on the searches where Amazon had been most visible. Our estimates imply that, had Amazon stayed, manufacturers’ presence in text ads would have been about 16% lower. Changes in competition, whether driven by platform design or by market structure, therefore spill over beyond the format where they originate.

A third paper asks what shoppers and firms gain from online-only retailers, such as marketplaces, in a market traditionally served by physical stores. Using vacuum cleaner sales in Belgium, Germany and Slovakia, we simulate the market without them: consumers would lose 12–17% of the gains they get from buying, and sellers 7–10% of theirs. Online-only retailers keep prices down and also reach people who would otherwise not buy at all.

Alongside the PhD I have worked as a research assistant at the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre, on the economics of digital technologies, and I am currently a research assistant at the Institut d’Economia de Barcelona.

Fields: Industrial Organization; Competition Policy; Digital Platforms and Advertising; E-commerce; Demand Estimation.

Research

Job Market Paper

The House Always Wins: Self-Preferencing After Google Shopping

with Néstor Duch-Brown (Joint Research Centre, European Commission)

May 2026 · JEL: L40, L41, L86, K21

The European Commission’s 2017 Google Shopping decision prohibited Google from favoring its own comparison shopping service but left the design of compliance to Google. Using web traffic data and an event study comparing European and non-European markets, we find that organic traffic to rival comparison shopping websites declined further after Google’s redesign of its results page, with larger losses for smaller platforms. The formally compliant remedy did not restore rivals’ visibility.

Working Papers

From Brick to Click: The Competitive and Welfare Effects of Digital Retail

with Néstor Duch-Brown (Joint Research Centre, European Commission)

JEL: L11, L13, L81

Using panel data on vacuum cleaner sales in Belgium, Germany and Slovakia (2015–2019), we estimate a random coefficients demand model to quantify the role of online-only retailers in a market traditionally served by click-and-mortar stores. Removing the online-only channel reduces consumer surplus by 12–17% and producer surplus by 7–10%: online-only retailers discipline prices and expand the market by reaching consumers who would otherwise not purchase.

Work in Progress

The Rival Next Door: Competition Across Search Advertising Formats

October 2026

We examine whether competition in one search advertising format affects firms’ entry into another. Exploiting Amazon’s exit from Google Shopping in July 2025, we find that text-ad entry rises in keywords where Amazon had a stronger presence in Shopping, with the response concentrated among manufacturers. A structural model of keyword entry shows that, had Amazon remained in Shopping, manufacturers’ participation in text advertising would have been about 16% lower.
